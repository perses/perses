# Origin of the name

Perses takes its name from a Titan of Greek mythology.

## Perses, the Titan

[Perses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perses_%28son_of_Crius%29) is a Titan, the son of Crius and Eurybia and the
brother of Astraeus and Pallas. His name is usually derived from the Greek verb *perthō*, "to destroy", yet the poet
Hesiod remembers him for something else: in his [Theogony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theogony), Perses is "eminent
among all men in wisdom".

Perses married his cousin [Asteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteria), "the starry one". To escape the advances of
Zeus, Asteria turned into a quail, *ortyx* in Greek, and leapt into the sea, where she became an island: Ortygia, the
"quail island", later known as Delos.

Their daughter is [Hecate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecate), the torch-bearing goddess of crossroads and magic,
whom Zeus honoured above all others.

## A cousin of Prometheus

Perses' father, Crius, is the brother of Iapetus, the father of [Prometheus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prometheus).
Perses and Prometheus are therefore cousins, a fitting family tie for a dashboard tool that grew up in the Prometheus
ecosystem.

If the name rings a different bell to Finnish or Estonian speakers, rest assured: the resemblance is a pure coincidence.
